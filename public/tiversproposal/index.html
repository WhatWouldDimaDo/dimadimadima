01 · Direction

Let’s start with the end in mind and work backwards.

The destination is more of the live, visible work Eric enjoys. Each part of the business should either create authority, generate demand, deepen transformation, or make delivery easier.

Working thesis

A book and signature talks create authority. Corporate programs monetize that authority. Coaching and community provide proof, depth, and continuity. AI and systems carry the research, preparation, packaging, and follow-through around the live work.

My recommendation: keep coaching and project work financially separate.

A clearly scoped paid project makes the value, expectations, time, and exit easier to manage. Any small demonstration included in discovery should be named and bounded rather than implying that the larger build is free.

Collaboration Principles

Make the relationship easy to understand.

Separate roles

Name when we are in a coaching conversation, project conversation, or potential authorship conversation.

Existing IP

Eric retains his content, brand, methods, and data; I retain my reusable tools, systems, and general know-how.

New work

Agree on ownership, license, credit, approval, and derivative use before creating a substantial asset.

Privacy by design

Consent, access, retention, local processing, and permitted use are included in the scope from the start.

Visible economics

Use a fixed fee, milestones, or retainer; add upside only where attribution and value can be measured.

Clean exit

Define handoff, deletion, continuing licenses, unfinished work, and public credit before either party needs them.

Choose the first question, then run a one-week Discovery Sprint.

The sprint is designed to create something concrete quickly, reveal what is feasible, and give us enough information to decide whether and how to work together next.

Decision session

Review the evidence, choose what to tackle first, and scope the next phase if useful.
