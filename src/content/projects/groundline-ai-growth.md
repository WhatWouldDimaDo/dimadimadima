## The Lightbulb

I showed a high-end landscaping client what image generation could do with an ordinary yard photo. The reaction was immediate: **what if a homeowner could see the potential of their own property before the first sales conversation?**

That visual gets attention. The larger opportunity is helping a great operator find promising work, make the possibility tangible, and learn what actually turns into revenue.

## What We Built

The first working version connects four simple pieces:

1. Find a property that could plausibly fit the client
2. Create a few grounded ways it might change
3. Turn those concepts into a personalized proposal
4. Track what happens next and use the result to improve the next campaign

The operator brings the judgment—what fits the customer, the craft, and the way the team actually works. I turn that judgment into something repeatable.

## What I Know So Far

The workflow is being used in real client work. It is **not yet proof of a repeatable customer-acquisition engine**, and there is no conversion claim.

That distinction matters. A strong visual is not a landscape plan. A candidate list is not a prediction model. The next useful learning will come from live campaigns, clean attribution, and conversations with more landscapers, pool builders, and outdoor contractors—not from adding more features in isolation.

## The Business I’m Exploring

Groundline is becoming a broader idea: an AI growth partner for builders and high-end trades.

The work starts by finding the opportunity together. Then we make it visible, build the first useful system, and leave the owner and team able to keep using and improving it. Depending on the business, that might mean better customer acquisition, a sharper sales experience, useful property intelligence, or less friction inside the operation.

That is the version of AI work I care about: the lightbulb moment, followed by something real.
