Atlanta to Charlotte with a toddler: every stop worth taking

The four hours between Atlanta and Charlotte on I-85 are exactly long enough to need two stops with a toddler and exactly boring enough that nobody's mapped where those stops should be. We drive this corridor to see family, my wife used to hand-build a Google map before every trip, and this guide is that map — verified, sourced, in order, northbound.

Ground rules: every claim below comes from an official parks page, a rest-area source, or a first-hand note, and the site listing for each stop shows the actual quote and link. Where we don't know something — and "is the playground fenced" is unknowable from the internet more often than you'd think — we say so instead of guessing.

Before you leave the Atlanta suburbs

Suwanee Town Center — Exit 111, about 2.5 miles off

Two stops in one, side by side, and they split the job cleanly:

  • PlayTown Suwanee is the playground: 13,000 square feet, built for all ages and abilities, with separate areas for toddlers and older kids — which matters when your two-year-old wants to shadow the big kids and you don't. Lighted pavilion with accessible picnic tables.
  • Suwanee Town Center Park, adjacent, has no playground — its listing says so outright — but it has the Big Splash Interactive Fountain: 43 jets, running daily 9:30am–9:30pm from April 1 to October 31. That's a longer season than every true splash pad on this route.

The Town Center restaurant district wraps around both, so this is also your best early food stop. If it's warm, pack the swim diaper somewhere you can reach it.

Spencer Park, Commerce — Exit 149, about 2 miles off

A small-town playground with a gazebo, confirmed on the city's parks page — and that's genuinely about all anyone can confirm. Restrooms unverified, picnic unverified. This is a leg-stretcher, not a destination; the Commerce/Tanger Outlets exit around it has food options. We list it because a modest, honest option at the right mile beats a great one at the wrong mile.

Lavonia City Park — Exit 173, off GA-17 behind City Hall

The last Georgia stop and a strong one: playground, splash pad (new around 2024), and restrooms — including restrooms near the splash pad, which anyone who has peeled a wet toddler knows is the detail that counts. Two pavilions, walking trails, a 4.8 Google rating across 86 reviews. This is a stop we found the parent way — my wife had it saved before any dataset existed.

The welcome-center gotcha at the state line

Here's the thing nobody tells you and every rest-area list gets wrong by omission: interstate rest areas serve one direction, and the two welcome centers at the GA/SC line are a mirrored pair.

  • The Georgia Welcome Center near Lavonia (mile 176) serves southbound traffic only. Driving Atlanta → Charlotte, you will watch it go by and you cannot stop there. It's also not 24 hours — restrooms run 7am to 11pm.
  • Your northbound stop is the South Carolina Welcome Center at Fair Play, just past the state line at exit 1 — open 24/7, with restrooms, vending, water, and picnic tables. No playground (the amenity list omits one), but for a diaper-and-coffee stop at highway speed, it's the reliable option on this stretch.

Plan around the pair: Fair Play on the way up, Lavonia on the way home — before 11pm.

South Carolina: the splash-pad stretch

Tyger River Park — Duncan, near I-85

A big county sports complex that happens to contain a toddler jackpot: multiple playgrounds, a 1.25-mile walking trail, picnic shelters, and a splash pad running 9am–8pm from Memorial Day to Labor Day. The public bathrooms by the Clubhouse building are described by a local parents' site as clean and well-stocked — a claim 2,279 Google reviewers (4.8 average) don't seem to dispute. Park hours are 7am–9pm in season. Roughly the trip's midpoint, and the stop we'd pick if we're only taking one.

Hyleman's Humble Playground — Gaffney, near I-85

The rarest thing on this corridor: a playground where "is it fenced?" has a documented answer. The playground and splash pad are fully fenced — stated plainly by a splash-pad directory that verifies such things — which means one adult can drink a coffee while the other doesn't sprint. It's an adaptive, sensory-rich playground built for kids of all abilities, with restrooms, on-site parking, and a seasonal splash pad running daily 10:00–7:30 from May through September. A 4.9 rating on 34 reviews — small-town numbers, big-deal stop.

North Carolina: the Gastonia cluster

Rick Murphrey Children's Park — Kings Mountain, at the YMCA

The second fenced entry: a safety fence encloses the playground with a single entrance — a design choice made by someone who has met a toddler. Fully accessible (high-backed harness swings, ramps to the play structures), with a splash park, restrooms, picnic shelters, and parking, all confirmed on the city's page.

Lineberger Park — Gastonia, on Garrison Blvd

A two-part park: big playground on the east end, sprayground on the west (11am–7pm daily, Memorial Day through Labor Day), with restroom buildings and water fountains on both ends and picnic shelters between. Dawn-to-dusk hours. There's also a separate paid pool with a lazy river on site if the stop accidentally becomes the afternoon. 1,594 reviews, 4.4 average — a real city park that absorbs a lot of Saturday.

Ranlo Park — Ranlo, Spencer Mountain Rd

The backup Gastonia-area option: modern playground (swings, slides, climbers), a splash pad next to the old Town Hall, and shaded picnic areas with grills. Honesty required by our own rules: restroom status is unverified — no source states it either way — so treat this as the "already stopped once for a bathroom, now they just need to run" stop.

Past Charlotte

Baker's Creek Park — Kannapolis

If you're continuing up I-85 beyond Charlotte, this 40-acre park has a playground in a shaded area, restrooms open during park hours, picnic shelters, and a greenway trailhead. One correction to what the internet implies: there's no splash pad — the water feature is the creek. Creek wading is its own kind of afternoon, but pack accordingly; it is not a push-button splash pad. 4.6 across 770 reviews.

What we left off, and why

Rhodes Jordan Park in Lawrenceville, GA shows up on every Atlanta-area list, and it's a genuinely good county park — playground, restrooms, pavilions, downtown restaurants adjacent. Two reasons it's not in the route list above: it's roughly 6–7 miles off I-85, past our detour cutoff, and its water feature is a paid pool complex, not a free splash pad — a distinction most listings blur and a toddler's swimsuit situation does not. Directories earn trust by what they decline to include.
